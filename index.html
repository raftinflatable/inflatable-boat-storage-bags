Specifying Inflatable Boat Storage Bags: Contents, Openings, Handles and Care

The storage bag is the part of an inflatable boat package that buyers think about least and handle most. Every trip starts by unzipping it and ends by stuffing a damp boat back in. If the opening is too small, the handles too weak or the bag too heavy to carry, the whole boat becomes harder to use. Raft Inflatable, the export catalog of Weihai Reachsea Outdoors Co., Ltd., gives a short specification method in its storage bag guide. This article expands it for fleet and distributor buyers.

Define what goes in each bag

The guide begins with contents. List the hull, floor, seats and accessories assigned to each bag. Measure the normal packed arrangement following the manual.

Then check whether rigid panels or long paddles need separate storage. The guide warns that forcing everything into one bag can create contact and compression problems. A sharp panel edge pressed against folded tube fabric for months is not a good idea.

For an aluminum or slatted floor, a separate panel bag is often practical. For an airmat floor, the floor may roll with the hull. Raft Inflatable's floor guidance explains how floor type changes packed size and setup.

Name the bags

When a boat ships with more than one bag, give each one a name and a contents list: "hull bag", "floor bag", "accessory bag". This makes packing, checking and replacing much simpler, especially in a fleet where many bags look identical.

Request practical dimensions

Catalog bag sizes are often external measurements. The guide asks for three more useful figures:

  1. Usable internal dimensions
  2. Opening size
  3. Loaded bag weight

It explains why: external measurements alone can hide a restrictive closure. A bag may be large enough on paper but have a zip opening that will not pass a folded hull.

Compare the bag with the narrowest vehicle or storage access point. The guide also recommends allowing for packing a used boat rather than a factory-tight bundle. A boat folded by hand on a beach rarely packs as small as one folded in a factory.

The FAQ adds a related point. Can shipping-carton dimensions be used to size a reusable bag? No. Use actual packed-component measurements and the proposed bag opening, not the carton alone.

Review handling features with the real users

Bags are carried by people, so the guide asks about handles, shoulder straps, closure and the intended carrying method for the named load. It warns against treating ordinary carry handles as approved hoisting points.

The most practical recommendation is to have a sample reviewed by the actual users along the planned storage-to-vehicle route. Raft Inflatable's launch and storage access guide describes how to survey that route: doors, stairs, slopes and distance.

Weight distribution

A single heavy bag may be manageable for two people and impossible for one. If the handling team is small, ask whether the load can be split across bags. Separate component weights from the supplier make this decision straightforward.

Include care and identification

The last step covers the bag itself. Request cleaning, drying and storage instructions for the bag. Keep wet-return drying separate from long-term storage.

The guide's FAQ makes the key point. Does a bag make wet storage acceptable? No. Follow the drying instructions before extended storage, because a closed bag can conceal retained moisture. Raft Inflatable's drying and storage guide and UV storage care guide cover the boat side of long-term storage.

Specify model labels and an inventory pocket if useful, and confirm replacement availability when ordering multiple boats. A lost or torn bag is a small problem if a matching replacement can be ordered by part identity.

A worked example

A rental operator orders twelve dinghies with aluminum floors. It asks for a hull bag and a separate floor-panel bag per boat, with usable internal dimensions, opening sizes and loaded weights. Each bag will carry a model label and an inventory pocket listing contents. Staff carry a sample set along the route from the store to the slipway. The opening on the hull bag proves tight for a used boat, so the supplier proposes a wider closure, confirmed in writing with a replacement bag part reference.

Procurement checklist

  • Assign contents to named bags.
  • Measure the opening and loaded package.
  • Check handling with the intended users.
  • Request bag care instructions and replacement availability.

Questions to send with the RFQ

Name the boat model and ask for: the number of bags supplied and the contents of each, usable internal dimensions and opening size per bag, loaded weight per bag, handle and strap arrangement with its intended carrying method, bag material care instructions, labeling options, and whether replacement bags can be ordered separately. Send your narrowest access dimension and the size of the handling team, so the supplier can propose a split that suits the real route. Raft Inflatable's request-quote page accepts these details in one written request, and the reply should be checked against a sample before the order is confirmed.

FAQ

Does a bag make wet storage acceptable?

No. Follow the drying instructions before extended storage; a closed bag can conceal retained moisture.

Can shipping-carton dimensions size a reusable bag?

No. Use actual packed-component measurements and the proposed bag opening.

Can bag handles be used to lift the boat by crane?

No. Ordinary carry handles are not approved hoisting points.

Should rigid floor panels share the hull bag?

Check first; forcing everything into one bag can cause contact and compression problems.
